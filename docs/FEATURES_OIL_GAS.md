# Oil and gas: features to build

The oil and gas side of the platform, on its own map page (`map.torvenco.com`, F-238). Asked by the owner on 2026-11-18: as the platform expands beyond grain, this page tracks oil and gas production across Canada. Built from the owner's study of the public data behind such a map (2026-11-18, kept by the owner); that study covers **Alberta only**, from free government sources.

**Direction (the owner, 2026-11-19):** the oil and gas side is a **map and data tool, not a CRM**. Its purpose is to show the oil and gas picture: the township grid, formation areas, wells (multi-leg wells among them), pipelines, Crown land, sales and the like. The aim is first to show what Offset Signal (offsetsignal.ca) shows, from the same public sources its own pages name (the owner's "Offset Signal — Data Source Breakdown", 2026-11-18), then to go further with new features. Built from the government sources only: never Offset Signal's data, wording or look. Oil and gas companies are a reference list of their own (F-244), never grain counterparties.

**The goal (the owner, 2026-11-20; it sets the order from here):** matching Offset Signal is not the aim; it is the base. What sets this tool apart is **analysis on the data**: financial forecasts, type curves, decline curves, Monte Carlo simulations, which Offset Signal does not have; later, a box where a person types a request in plain language (the owner's example: "Show me all of the abandoned wells in this area and a historical type curve of all of their combined productions") and the map and charts are set accordingly. That needs **all the data of an area in our own database, history included**: wells planned, drilling, producing, suspended, closed and abandoned, and each well's production month by month. So the fastest MVP is **one small formation, whole**, chosen from a button, rather than a little of everything across Alberta: "Fastest MVP" below. The "Build order (2026-11-19)" further down is kept for the record and for what comes after the MVP.

**What it is for (the owner, 2026-11-19):** the owner's own side project, not oil or gas trading: an MVP to show to friends in the industry and let them try. Built fast under the light process (`AGENTS.md`, "Working with the owner"); robustness work comes only if they want it in their companies. **Priority:** F-243 part 4, the wells and their curved drilled paths on the map, from a one-off manual ST37 load; the monthly ST37 loading job is postponed until there is real demand.

Same rules as `docs/FEATURES.md` (IDs never reused, every request recorded before code, statuses as there) and as the rest of the platform: nothing invented; every dataset shows its source and date; **each source's terms of use and any price are read from its own published page and recorded in `docs/DECISIONS.md` before it is used** (the links and terms below are the study's findings, not yet checked by an agent); sources are fetched by the Worker, never the browser; no data copied from other companies' compiled products, only from the government sources themselves.

## Fastest MVP: one formation, whole (the owner, 2026-11-20)

**Asked:** the owner, 2026-11-20: "show one of the small formations in its entirety … a button to show the data for one of them"; all its data brought into our database, the historical data too, so that forecasts, type curves, declines and Monte Carlo simulations can run on it, and later a plain-language box. The steps below are an agent's proposal at that request, each ending with something to see; nothing is built until the owner answers the questions at the end.

**Why one formation:** the analysis needs complete data, not wide data. A type curve from half of a formation's wells is wrong; one small formation loaded whole is right, small enough for the free database plan (each load measured, the 400 MB guard kept), and the same steps then repeat for the next formation.

**Steps (F-252, then F-253, then F-254):**
1. **Choose the formation (F-252).** An agent first counts, from the AER's own files, the wells of a few small candidates the owner names (or of the smallest ones), with each one's size in the database, for the owner to choose. Needs the AER's code list of fields and pools (ST103) to tell which wells belong to which formation, and a check of what ST37 itself says about each well's field and pool.
2. **Every well of that formation, whatever its status (F-252).** The **Load ST37 wells** workflow given a formation instead of a rectangle: licensed, drilling, producing, suspended, closed and abandoned wells, with their drilled paths and legs. Licensed wells as dashed planned lines (F-243 part 4b) are built here, as they are the "upcoming drilling" of the formation. Upcoming licences and rigs come from the daily lists already kept (F-243 parts 1 and 2), filtered to the formation. To see: a **formation button** on the map that shows that formation alone, with its counts by status.
3. **Each well's production, month by month (F-252).** Oil, gas and water volumes and hours on production, as far back as the public source goes. **Source to be confirmed before any code:** the study names none yet; the candidates an agent knows of are Petrinex's public Alberta data (monthly volumes by well) and the AER's own production reports. Their terms of use, how far back they reach, their file sizes and any price are read from their own pages and recorded in `docs/DECISIONS.md` first (the rule at the top of this file). To see: a well's card with its production chart; the formation's total production by month.
4. **Type curves and declines (F-253).** For the wells in view or chosen by filters (status, operator, spud year, the map's window): their production lined up from each well's first month, the average curve with its spread (the type curve), a fitted decline with its numbers, and the combined historical production. Every figure is computed from the stored volumes, with the formula and its version shown; nothing estimated without saying so.
5. **Financial forecasts and Monte Carlo (F-253).** A forecast of volumes from the fitted decline, then money: prices, costs, royalties and the discount rate are **typed by the person** (or come from a source the owner names), never made up by an agent (`AGENTS.md` rule 1); Monte Carlo runs draw the decline's numbers and the prices from ranges the person sets, and show the spread of results (for example the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles). Decimal arithmetic for money, tests for every formula.
6. **The plain-language box (F-254).** The person types a request; Claude turns it into **settings from a fixed list** (which statuses, area, operator, years; which chart), which the page then applies as if the person had clicked them, and shows which settings it chose. Claude writes no numbers and reads no data: every figure still comes from the database and the formulas of steps 4 and 5. One request to Anthropic's API from the Worker, no tools, no agent framework (`AGENTS.md` rule 9; the same pattern as the company summaries).

**Set aside until after the MVP:** land sales, Crown agreements and freehold minerals stored (F-241, F-242), companies and their pages (F-244, F-245), the whole province's wells, other provinces (F-246), pipelines (F-249), the monthly loading jobs. Live layers stay available in Settings.

**Found on 2026-11-20 (an agent, from the AER's and Petrinex's own pages and files; nothing built on it yet):**
- **Which wells are Duvernay.** ST37's production strings carry a pool code; the AER's ST103 lists (`https://www.aer.ca/data-and-performance-reports/statistical-reports/st103`, monthly; `FieldPoolList.txt` and `FormationCodes_NumericSort.txt` under `https://www.aer.ca/data/codes/`) show that a pool code is a four-digit formation code followed by the pool's number, and that formation codes 7280 to 7288 are the Duvernay (7285 "Duvernay-Majeau Lake"; 7190 "Duvernay (old)" has no wells in ST37). Counted in the ST37 file downloaded that day: **about 1,990 wells** in Duvernay pools, in 47 fields: about 1,910 producing, about 50 suspended, about 25 abandoned, the rest drilled or testing; none confidential; first drilled 2010, about 700 of them since 2022.
- **Licensed and drilling Duvernay wells cannot be told from ST37 alone:** a well has a pool code only once it has a production string. The formation a licence aims at is in the AER's daily licence list (ST1, kept since 2026-11-19 only) and in Petrinex's public **Well Licence** file (every licence, with its projected and terminating formation, drilling operation and projected depth; made nightly). So the planned lines of part 4b, for the Duvernay, need that file.
- **Monthly production by well: Petrinex's public data** (`https://www.petrinex.ca/public-data/apd/`): **Conventional Volumetric Data**, one file a month (about 8 MB zipped, 128 MB as a table), at `https://www.petrinex.gov.ab.ca/publicdata/API/Files/AB/Vol/<year>-<month>/CSV`; each line is a facility, month, activity and product, and a well's own production appears as lines whose "from" is the well (oil, gas, water, condensate, with hours); wells are tied to their pool by Petrinex's **Well Infrastructure** file. Too large for the Worker: loaded by a GitHub workflow, as ST37 is.
- **Only five years are free.** Petrinex gives "current production year plus the past four production years" (the earliest month that answered: 2022-01); older months "can be requested from the AER … Applicable AER Fees apply". The AER's catalogue (`https://www1.aer.ca/ProductCatalogue/514.html`, "Well Production Data", from 1962): CAD 10 for one well, CAD 74 for one field and pool or one township, CAD 750 for the whole province's file of one month, CAD 6,000 for 12 issues. So free data gives the full history of the roughly 700 Duvernay wells drilled since 2022, and only the later years of the roughly 1,300 drilled before: their first years, which a type curve needs most, are missing unless bought.
- **Petrinex's terms** (`https://www.petrinex.ca/terms`, read 2026-11-20): the material "is owned by the Government of Alberta"; it "may be used, reproduced, stored or transmitted for non-commercial purposes", Crown copyright acknowledged; for commercial purposes, "arrange first for consent". Not the Open Government Licence. The same question as the AER's terms: the owner's decision, to record in `docs/DECISIONS.md` before any Petrinex file is used.

**The owner's decisions (2026-11-20, recorded in `docs/DECISIONS.md`):** 1. Petrinex's files are used ("This isn't for commercial use at all"), on the AER's conditions, Crown copyright named. 2. The free years first.

## Two things that shape the order

1. **Showing is cheap; storing is not.** The province publishes several of these datasets as live ArcGIS map services, and the platform already shows live ArcGIS layers, fetched by the Worker by map square (F-107, F-141). Those can appear on the map with no database work (F-240, F-241). Searching, history, totals and joins (who bought what, which wells sit on which land) need the data stored, and at Alberta's size (hundreds of thousands of wells, every agreement tract, sales since 2003) that will not fit the Supabase free plan's database (a cost: the owner's decision).
2. **Some files are too large for the Worker.** The AER's full well list (ST37) is a 1.23 GB geodatabase or a 170 MB Excel package, monthly; the Worker has 128 MB of memory. Loading it needs another kind of job (for example a scheduled GitHub Actions run), an architecture decision (`AGENTS.md` rule 9) before building F-243.
